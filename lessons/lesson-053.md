## Important concepts

### Words people remember

People build passwords from company names, products, local terms and personal details. A wordlist scraped from an organization's public site captures that vocabulary, which is why it can outperform a generic breach list for a specific target.

### Passive collection

CEWL reads publicly accessible pages. It does not attack the site, but crawling still generates requests and should stay within scope and reasonable rate limits.

## Practical notes

Crawl a site you own or are authorized to assess, and save the output outside the repository.

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `cewl https://example.com -d 2 -m 5 -w words.txt` | Crawl two levels deep, keep words of five or more characters, save to a file. |
| `cewl https://example.com -e -w words-meta.txt` | Include email addresses found on the site. |
| `wc -l words.txt` | Check how many candidates the crawl produced. |

Commands are shown for study. Run them only in your own lab, read the manual page (`man <tool>`) first, and check every option before executing anything.

## FAQ

### What does the -m option do in CEWL?

It sets the minimum word length, filtering out short noise that is unlikely to be useful.

### Can CEWL replace rockyou.txt?

No. It produces a targeted supplement. Combining a targeted list with a broad list often works best.
