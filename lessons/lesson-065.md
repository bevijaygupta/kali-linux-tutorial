## Important concepts

### The race to answer first

DNS spoofing works by supplying a forged answer before or instead of the legitimate one, or by poisoning a resolver's cache. The victim then connects to the address the attacker chose while the domain name looks correct.

### Why HTTPS still matters

Redirecting DNS does not automatically defeat TLS. A fake site without a valid certificate triggers a browser warning. Users who click through warnings, or sites that serve mixed or unencrypted content, remain exposed.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between DNS spoofing and DNS cache poisoning?

Spoofing supplies a forged response to a query. Cache poisoning inserts a forged record into a resolver's cache so many users receive it.

### How do I defend against DNS spoofing?

Use DNSSEC where available, encrypted DNS, trusted resolvers, static entries for critical hosts, and network controls that prevent ARP manipulation.
