## About this chapter

Chapter 32 begins the ethical hacking methodology with footprinting, the information-gathering phase that happens before any active testing. It separates passive from active footprinting, shows what kinds of information are collected, and explains where that information comes from and why it shapes every later phase.

## What you will learn

- What footprinting is and where it fits in a penetration test
- The difference between passive and active footprinting
- What information is collected about an organization
- Which public sources reveal domain, infrastructure and people data
- How footprinting feeds scanning, enumeration and exploitation
- Why scope and authorization must be defined first

## Important concepts

### Passive first

Passive footprinting uses public information without touching the target: search engines, social media, public records, certificate transparency and DNS history. It is quiet and low risk. Active footprinting sends traffic to target systems and must stay inside an authorized scope.

### Scope is a technical control

A penetration test starts with a written scope that lists allowed targets, time windows and prohibited techniques. Footprinting often reveals related domains and third parties; discovering them does not authorize testing them.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between footprinting and scanning?

Footprinting gathers information from public and semi-public sources. Scanning actively probes systems to discover hosts, ports and services.

### Is footprinting legal?

Using public sources is generally legal. Active footprinting and any testing of target systems require authorization.
